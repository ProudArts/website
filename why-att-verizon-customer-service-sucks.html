My Theory on Why AT&T and Verizon Customer Service Sucks

By John Daniels • Published on ProudArts.com

I have been reading through the comments in this group and across the web, and almost everyone is asking the same question: how can a massive telecom giant with unlimited money, top engineers, and endless resources have the worst customer support in America? How is it logical that you call in, get bounced from department to department for hours, and nobody cares enough to fix a simple problem?

After looking up AT&T's Board of Directors on their website and seeing how the corporate world is wired, I have a theory on why this happens.

First, look at the people sitting at the top. If you check the bios of the board members running these corporations, it is a tight circuit. They come from the same Ivy League pipelines and bounce from one Fortune 500 boardroom to the next. They are insulated from regular people and have zero skin in the game when an everyday customer's phone stops working.

Second, follow the money. Because AT&T is in the S&P 500, billions of dollars automatically pour into its stock every two weeks from teachers' pensions, firefighters' retirements, and 401(k) index funds managed by giants like BlackRock, Vanguard, and State Street. Those institutions have to buy and hold the stock no matter how badly the company treats its customers.

Third, there is no real competition left. Between AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile, three companies control almost the entire wireless market, and the exact same Wall Street investment giants are the top shareholders in all of them. That is why Verizon's support feels identical to AT&T's.

When a company has guaranteed retirement money flowing in every paycheck cycle, the same institutional landlords at the top, and a near monopoly where customers have nowhere else to go, there is zero financial incentive to spend money on good customer service. To them, support is not about helping people, it is a cost to be gutted and outsourced because the money rolls in either way.
